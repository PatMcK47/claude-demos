1. UNIT OF OBSERVATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  The unit of observation is the ECONOMIC FAMILY UNIT.

  An economic family is defined as a group of two or more persons who
  live in the same dwelling and are related by blood, marriage,
  common-law union, or adoption. Persons living alone or with
  unrelated others are treated as "unattached individuals" — each
  constituting a separate record.

  Every row in the PUMF represents one family unit. Individual-level
  data (age, sex, education of the major income earner) is rolled up
  to the family-unit level. The file does not contain person-level
  records.

  Approximate PUMF sample size: ~10,400 family units (2019 cycle).
  Estimated target population:  ~15.6 million Canadian family units.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PURPOSE OF DATA COLLECTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  The SFS collects information on the assets, debts, employment,
  income, and education of Canadian families. Its stated purpose is
  to provide a comprehensive picture of the net worth — or financial
  health — of Canadians.

  Key policy uses include:
    - Measuring wealth inequality across demographic groups
    - Assessing the financial preparedness of Canadians for retirement
    - Understanding household indebtedness and financial vulnerability
    - Tracking the distribution of assets and debts over time
    - Informing housing, pension, and social policy

  The SFS is conducted every three years (2016, 2019, 2023, ...).
  It is a voluntary survey with a response rate of roughly 43–70%
  depending on the cycle.

3. KEY VARIABLES
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The PUMF contains approximately 100 variables. Below are the key
substantive variables organized by category. Variable names follow
the PUMF naming convention (prefix "P" = PUMF-level).

--- A. Survey Weight ---

  PWEIGHT           Survey (sampling) weight — must be applied for
                    population-representative estimates. Each record's
                    weight indicates how many family units in the
                    population that record represents.

--- B. Geography ---

  PPROVRES          Province of residence (10 provinces; territories
                    excluded from the survey frame)
  PREGION           Region of residence (grouped provinces for
                    disclosure protection)

--- C. Demographics of the Major Income Earner ---

  PEFMJSEX          Sex of the major income earner
  PEFMJAGE          Age of the major income earner (continuous)
  PAGEMIEG           Age group (categorical)
  PEDUCMIE           Highest level of education
  PMATSTAT           Marital status
  PIMMIG             Immigration status (recent cycles)
  PMTHERLN           Mother tongue

--- D. Family Characteristics ---

  PEFSIZE           Economic family size (number of persons)
  PFMTYPG           Family type (couple with/without children,
                    lone parent, unattached individual)
  PNBREAR           Number of income earners

--- E. Income ---

  PFMTINC           Total family (market) income
  PEFATINC           After-tax family income
  PFEMPLOY           Employment income
  PFINCOME           Investment/financial income
  PGVTRANS           Government transfer income
  PFMINCG            Family income group (categorical)

--- F. Assets —  Non-Financial ---

  PWAPRVAL           Value of principal residence
  PWASTRST           Value of other real estate (cottages, rental
                     properties, land)
  PWAVHCLE           Value of vehicles
  PWABUSEQ           Business equity
  PWACNTNT           Household contents and personal effects

--- G. Assets — Financial ---

  PATDEP             Bank deposits (chequing, savings, GICs)
  PATRRSP            RRSPs (Registered Retirement Savings Plans)
  PATRRIF            RRIFs (Registered Retirement Income Funds)
  PATTFSA            TFSAs (Tax-Free Savings Accounts; 2012+)
  PATRESP            RESPs (Registered Education Savings Plans)
  PATSTCK            Stocks (publicly traded)
  PATMUTF            Mutual funds / investment funds (non-registered)
  PATEPP             Employer pension plan (RPP) value

--- H. Asset Totals ---

  PATOTAST / PWASTTOT   Total assets (financial + non-financial)
  PATFEQ                Total financial assets
  PATNFEQ               Total non-financial assets

--- I. Debts ---

  PDTMRPR            Mortgage on principal residence
  PDTMORE            Mortgage on other real estate
  PDTVHCLE           Vehicle loans
  PDTSLOA            Student loans
  PDTCRCL            Credit card debt (outstanding balance)
  PDTLOC             Line of credit balance
  PDTBUS             Business debts
  PDTOTHER           Other debts (taxes owing, past-due bills)
  PDTTOTDT           Total debts

--- J. Net Worth ---

  PWNETWPG           Net worth, going-concern basis
  PWNETWPT           Net worth, termination basis
  PNTWRTHQ           Net worth quintile (categorical)
  PNTWRTHD           Net worth decile (categorical)

  Note: "Going-concern" values employer pensions assuming the plan
  continues; "termination" values them as if the plan wound up today.

--- K. Housing ---

  POWNRENT           Home ownership status (owner vs. renter)
  PPRSTYPE           Dwelling type
  PPRSYRAQ           Year principal residence was acquired
  PPRSDOWN           Down payment on principal residence

--- L. Financial Attitudes & Behaviours ---

  PFINSITX           Expected financial situation (better/same/worse)
  PSAVHAB            Saving habits / net saver indicator
  PFINSTRS           Financial stress indicator

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. NOTES ON USING THE PUMF
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Always apply the survey weight (PWEIGHT) for population estimates.
  - The PUMF does NOT include bootstrap weights; use CV guidelines
    and minimum cell sizes (n >= 25) for reliability assessment.
  - Some variables are top-coded or grouped for disclosure protection.
  - For full-detail microdata with bootstrap weights, apply to a
    Statistics Canada Research Data Centre (RDC).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
6. HOW TO GENERATE FULL STATISTICS FROM THE MICRODATA
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  Step 1 — Download the data:
      cd sfs && python3 download_sfs_pumf.py

  Step 2 — Run the EDA script:
      python3 eda.py

  The eda.py script will:
    - Auto-detect the CSV/TXT data file in sfs/data/
    - Print unweighted and weighted descriptive statistics
    - Generate frequency tables for demographic variables
    - Overwrite this report file with results from the actual data
